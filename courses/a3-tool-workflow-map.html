The tool and workflow map

Every vendor demo shows you a screen appearing out of a sentence. None of them tell you the thing you actually need to decide a purchase: what artifact lands on your side at the end, and whether your pipeline can accept it. Tonight we map five prompt-to-UI tools onto a real design pipeline, then read the gates that decide whether adoption is even possible in your org: seats, credits, training defaults, indemnity scope. Every number on this page comes from vendor documentation, with the contradictions named rather than smoothed over.

Part 1 · the only question that matters

What artifact does it produce, and where does that artifact land? 12 min live

Tool evaluations go wrong because leaders compare demos. Demos are all impressive and all interchangeable. The question that separates a purchase from a toy is boring and answerable: at the end of the session, what exists, and can the next person in your pipeline pick it up? A hosted URL is not a handoff. A pile of React is not a design system. A beautiful page you cannot export is a destination, not a stage. Here is the same five tools, described by output rather than by promise, from their own documentation.

LiveThe four sentences to say in a vendor call4 min▶

Skip the demo. Ask these, and write the answers down, because you will need them again at renewal:

  • "Show me the export." Not the preview. The zip, the repo, the file. If there is no documented export, you are buying a destination and should staff it as a channel, not as a stage.
  • "Which direction does the sync run?" Figma Make's GitHub push is one-way by documentation: edits made in GitHub do not come back and are overwritten on the next push. That single sentence decides who owns the code.
  • "Does the output arrive tied to our design system?" Figma's own docs say pasted-back Make layers are not automatically tied to your design system. v0 handles this differently, through a stored "skill" adapter that names where your components, props and tokens live.
  • "What does one real task cost in your metering unit?" Not the plan price. The per-task cost, at your team's volume. Part 2 is entirely about this.
Self-studyFree-tier gates that quietly decide your pilot3 min read▶

Pilots fail on gates, not on capability. From the vendors' own documentation:

  • v0 Free is 7 messages per day, and Figma import needs a paid plan. A seven-message ceiling is not a pilot, it is a taste test.
  • Lovable prices by credits but allows unlimited workspace members, which inverts the usual seat maths: you can invite the whole team and still be constrained by throughput.
  • Framer plans are per-site with a workspace-shared credit pool. Two sites, two plans, one pool of generation.
  • Figma Make code editing needs a Full seat on a paid plan. Starter plans do not include Figma AI at all.

Write the gate next to each tool on your shortlist before you write the price. Half the shortlist usually disqualifies itself.

Part 2 · whether adoption is even possible

Seats, credits and training defaults 16 min live

Capability is not the constraint any more. Metering is. Every major vendor has moved to a credit model, and credits are per seat, per month, mostly without rollover and mostly not shareable. That turns a design decision into a capacity-planning decision, and it is the part of the purchase most design leaders discover after signing. Below are the gates as documented, per vendor, with the numbers that actually bind.

LiveFigma - seats first, then credits6 min▶

The seat gate. Figma AI features generally require a Full seat on a paid plan. View, Collab and Dev seats can try them in Draft files. Starter plans do not include Figma AI. If your extended team sits on Starter or on View seats, the rollout you are imagining does not exist yet.

The credit gate. AI credits are the real constraint. They renew monthly, do not roll over, and are not shareable.

PlanCredits per Full seat / monthNote
Starter500With a 150 per day cap. Starter does not include Figma AI

What things cost. Zero-credit features: AI search and Rename layers. Sample documented costs: remove background 1 to 5, vectorize 2 to 5, boost resolution 5 to 10, Make image 2 to 16, Add interactions 20, and Figma Make at 30 to 100 or more per task. Pay-as-you-go is $0.03 per credit with a spend cap. Features in beta consume 0 credits, which is the cheapest window you will get and also the least stable one.

Do the arithmetic out loud. A Professional Full seat gets 3,000 credits. At 100 credits per Figma Make task, that is roughly 30 Make tasks a month for that person, before a single image edit. Say that number in the meeting where someone proposes "everyone prototypes in Make now".

LiveFigma - the current feature names, so you stop buying last year's roadmap3 min▶

Names change faster than procurement cycles, and asking for a retired feature makes you easy to sell to. As of the verification date:

  • First Draft is the current name. It is not "Make Design". "AI text suggestions" is retired.
  • Figma agent (beta from 2026-05-20) is becoming the entry point for First Draft functionality. Config 2026 additions in open beta: custom skills, web search, file attachments, verified partner MCP connectors.
  • Figma Make reached GA on 2026-07-24, with publishing still in beta.
  • Figma Motion, custom shader effects, generative plugins and Weave tools are in open beta. Code layers are in closed beta.
  • Other current features: Rename layers, Replace content, Add interactions, rewrite / translate / shorten, Adjust tone, Make image, Edit image with prompt, Remove background, Expand image, Isolate or erase object, Vectorize image, Boost resolution, AI search.
  • Figma Make's model picker names Gemini, Claude and GPT models. The default is deliberately unnamed and documented as something that "may change over time" - worth knowing if you have a model-approval process.
LiveAdobe, Canva, Midjourney - the gates that bind5 min▶

Adobe. Firefly Image Model 5 is generally available, with native 4MP generation, and it powers Prompt to Edit. Firefly Audio and Speech models are in public beta; Firefly Custom Models went public beta in March 2026 and are private by default; Firefly Boards has been GA worldwide since 2025-09-24. Firefly now hosts 30 or more partner models (Google, Runway, Kling, OpenAI, Black Forest Labs, ElevenLabs, Topaz, Moonvalley) alongside Adobe's own. In the apps: Photoshop has Generative Fill, Generative Expand, Remove Tool, Harmonize and Reference Image for Generative Fill, all GA at 2K output, with a model picker that includes partner models; Illustrator has Generative Recolor, Generative Shape Fill and Text to Pattern / Generate Patterns. Generative credits renew monthly and most standard features cost 1 credit per generation, while premium features cost far more - and partner-model output is billed as premium. That last sentence is both a cost fact and a rights fact, which is why it comes back in Part 3.

Canva. The naming has moved from "Magic" to Canva AI. Still-current Magic names: Magic Media, Magic Write, Magic Grab, Magic Video, Magic Edit, Magic Design, Magic Resize. "Magic Switch" is no longer a help-centre feature - its functions are documented as Magic Resize, Translate and Transform into Doc, so if your enablement deck says Magic Switch, it is out of date. Free versus paid is now one shared credit pool across three quality tiers (Standard, Premium, Ultra) rather than per-feature counts: Free gets 200 Standard or 20 Premium and no Ultra; Pro and Teams 2,000 / 200 / 20; Business and Enterprise 4,000 / 400 / 40. Credits are per member and never pooled. Magic Resize is metered and not available on Free. Canva Teams is closed to new sign-ups, so the ladder you are actually buying is Free, Pro, Business, Enterprise.

Midjourney. V8.2 is the current default (since 2026-07-24). Plans meter fast GPU hours: Basic $10 (3.3 fast GPU hours, roughly 200 minutes, no Relax), Standard $30 (15 hours plus unlimited Relax), Pro $60 (30 hours, Stealth), Mega $120 (60 hours, Stealth). Fast time does not roll over. Commercial terms are the same across tiers except that companies grossing over $1M USD per year must be on Pro or Mega. If your company is over that line, the $10 plan is not a cheap pilot, it is a licence breach.

Self-studyDesign-system automation has a harder gate than any of these3 min read▶

If your plan is "AI generates screens from our tokens", one documented limit decides your architecture: the Figma variables REST API is Enterprise-only - you need a Full seat in an Enterprise org. Below Enterprise, the only token route is selection-scoped get_variable_defs through the Figma MCP server (remote https://mcp.figma.com/mcp, or desktop http://127.0.0.1:3845/mcp). That is a per-selection read, not a systems integration.

One more date for the calendar: Figma Code Connect framework-specific parsers lose updates and support from 2026-08-17, after which template files are the only maintained path. Code Connect itself needs a Dev or Full seat on Organization or Enterprise. Designer session b6 works through all of this at the practitioner level; you need it here only to size the programme honestly.

Part 3 · three things to read before adopting

Commercial safety, indemnity and the training default 11 min live

There are exactly three passages you must read in a vendor's own words before you let a tool touch client work. Not a summary, not the sales sheet: the vendor's documentation, on the day you sign. Each one has a carve-out that matters more than the headline.

Live1 · The commercial-safety claim, and what it excludes4 min▶

Adobe's own framing for the first Firefly model is that it was trained on Adobe Stock images, openly licensed content and public-domain content where copyright has expired, and that it is "designed to generate images safe for commercial use". Read that carefully. "Designed to be" is a design goal, not a warranty. It describes the training-data intent, and intent is not the same as an indemnity you can point a client at.

The carve-out is the part that gets skipped. For the 30 or more partner models now hosted inside Firefly, Adobe states it cannot verify commercial safety and points you to the partner's terms. So the same window, the same subscription, the same button - and two completely different rights positions depending on which model is selected in the picker. Combine that with the billing fact from Part 2 (partner-model output is billed as premium) and you get a usefully blunt rule of thumb: if it cost extra, check whose terms apply.

What to do with this: name the models your team is allowed to select, in writing, and make the model picker a governed choice rather than a convenience.

Live2 · The shape of an indemnification4 min▶

Indemnification is purchased, not automatic. In Adobe's framing, enterprise customers "may purchase an entitlement that comes with contractual IP indemnification for select Firefly outputs" - and that entitlement is scoped by published lists of eligible features, surfaces and export events. Three scoping dimensions, any one of which can put your asset outside the cover:

  • Feature - which generative feature produced it.
  • Surface - which app or product it was produced in.
  • Export event - how it left the tool.

Add the two standing exclusions this session has already established: partner-model output and beta features. Beta is doubly seductive because betas consume 0 Figma credits and often carry the newest capability, so the cheapest and shiniest path is frequently the least covered one.

An honest gap, stated as a gap: the exact conditions of Adobe's enterprise indemnity could not be verified during preparation, because the enterprise legal FAQ document was unreachable. Do not let anyone, including this page, tell you the precise terms. Get them from your contract.

Live3 · The training default nobody was asked about3 min▶

Figma has two toggles: Content training and AI features. The default for content training is on for Starter and Professional teams, and off and not toggleable for Organization and Enterprise. The policy took effect on 2024-08-15. Education and Government accounts are excluded.

Read that as an org-design fact rather than a privacy fact. It means the small teams least likely to have a policy are the ones opted in by default - the contractor's Professional team, the agency's Starter file, the side project where your unreleased brand work lives. Your Enterprise org is protected; your supply chain may not be.

Canva's position is different in shape: its documentation says "you own your Output, except for any Output that modifies or incorporates Licensed Content", warns that outputs may not be unique, and recommends independent advice before commercial use. Training use is governed by Privacy Settings. Three different vendors, three different defaults, one shared consequence: the default is the policy, until someone changes it.

Two honesty notes to carry into the meeting Official vendor pages sometimes contradict each other. Figma's own pages disagree on whether the agent beta is Enterprise-only or available to Full seats on Pro, Organization and Enterprise, and there is no point pretending one of them is authoritative. Separately, a live "unlimited generations" promotion can distort Adobe credit arithmetic badly enough that any number you teach today may be meaningless next month. Verify both against today's page and against your own contract, and say "verified on this date" whenever you quote a number.
Real world

Run the frame on Cadence's own marketing site. Gate 1: Framer AI produces a published Framer site and no documented code export, which is fine, because a marketing site is a destination and always was. Gate 2: it does not touch the product design system, so the risk is brand drift rather than system drift. Gate 3: imagery is where the rights question lives, not layout. Gate 4: per-site plans with a workspace-shared credit pool means two sites share one pool. Verdict: adopt for the site, ignore for the product. That is a five-minute decision once you ask about artifacts instead of features.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 40-50 min total

  • Take the three tools your team already uses and fill in one row each of the Part 1 table from the vendor's own documentation: real artifact, where it lands, honest limit. Note the date you verified it.
  • Do the credit arithmetic for one person on your actual plan. Credits per seat, divided by the cost of the task they do most. Write the monthly task count as a single number and take it to your next budget conversation.
  • Check one training default. Find the content-training toggle on every Figma team your work touches, including contractors and agencies, and record which ones default to on.
  • Pick one tool currently used on client work and answer gate 3 in writing: which feature, which surface, which export event, and is partner-model output involved. If you cannot answer, that is your finding.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Your team needs a generated draft to reach an engineer as maintainable code. Which tool's documented output does not support that?

Framer AI produces a destination, not a handoff. That is a legitimate choice for a marketing site and a dead end for a product pipeline. Figma Make sits in between: there is a code zip and a GitHub push, but the push is one-way and pasted-back layers are not automatically tied to your design system.

2 · A designer generates a hero image inside Firefly using one of the hosted partner models. What is the rights position?

The commercial-safety framing applies to Adobe's own model, and even there it is a design goal rather than a warranty. Partner-model output is explicitly outside it, and indemnification is a purchased entitlement scoped to eligible features, surfaces and export events, with partner-model and beta output excluded.

3 · Your team is on Figma Professional, Full seats. Why is "everyone prototypes in Make now" a capacity decision rather than a licence decision?

Starter is the plan that excludes Figma AI. On Professional the seat is fine and the meter is the problem: 3,000 credits divided by a task costing 30 to 100 or more is roughly 30 to 100 Make tasks a month per person, before any image work. Credits do not roll over and are not shareable, so unused capacity on one seat cannot rescue another.
